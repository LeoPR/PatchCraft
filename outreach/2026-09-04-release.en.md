## What the library is

It cuts an image into patches and puts it back. One image per call, one tensor in and one
tensor out. There is no batch axis, no dataset, no dataloader and no model, and that
boundary is written as binding in `docs/THEORY.md` §0.

`pip install patchcraft` · MIT · pre-1.0 · Python 3.12 to 3.14 · torch 2.6 or newer.

## The headlines, in the order that matters to someone who does not know the project

### 1. The two defects that do not announce themselves

They are why the library exists, and the reader may be carrying one of them right now.

The first is the reshape. `F.unfold` returns `(1, C*ph*pw, L)`, and the intuitive reshape
into `(L, C, ph, pw)` gives the right shape with the wrong pixels. The shape `assert`
passes, training runs, the loss falls a little less, and nothing complains.

The second is a stride that does not cover the image. On a 128 by 128 image with patch 32
and stride 20, the grid stops at pixel 112 and leaves 3840 of the 16384 pixels at zero. A
hand-rolled `fold` returns that partly black image without an error.

Neither is hard to fix. Both are easy to miss, and that difference is what justifies
writing it once with tests around it.

### 2. A numeric contract the caller can evaluate before calling

The round trip is bit-exact **if and only if every value in the coverage count map is a
power of two**. Outside that, the per-pixel error is bounded by `(k+1)·eps·|v|`, with `k`
that pixel's coverage count.

There is one reason: dividing a float by a power of two is the only division that never
rounds. `stride == patch_size` always satisfies it, because every count is 1.

What makes this a contract rather than a promise is the second half: the condition is
computed from the geometry alone, without running anything. The caller knows which regime
they are in beforehand.

### 3. The suite whose job is to bring the contract down

A test whose explicit purpose is to falsify the claim above. It enumerates the 126,736
legal geometries of the space **without consulting the predicate**, so that the enumerator
and the thing under test stay independent, and it hunts the two counterexamples: a case
inside the rule that is not exact, and a case outside it that is exact by luck. The full
sweep sits behind `PATCHCRAFT_SWEEP_FULL=1`.

It exists in that shape for a concrete reason, and this is where the retraction belongs, as
provenance rather than as a headline: an earlier version of this contract was published,
measured, found false and corrected in public. It said the error outside the rule stayed
around 1 ULP, when it reaches 19 ULP in float32. The old rule looked at the maximum of the
coverage map; over 14,969 rectangular geometries it mispredicts 3,936 cases, while the
power-of-two rule mispredicts 8, all of them in the safe direction of promising less than
it delivers.

And the suite missed it because the tests built their images with `torch.arange`. Integer
data round-trips exactly where random data does not. The test passed because it was asking
the wrong question with great confidence, and that is the part that transfers to any other
project.

### 4. The native accelerator, and why the gain is not a core count

Five of the six wheels carry a Rust kernel for the overlapping fold, compiled into the
wheel itself. There is no extra to enable.

| Geometry | Call | Pure torch | Accelerated | Speedup |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3x512x512, patch 32, stride 16 | `reconstruct` | 16.3 ms | 2.3 ms | 7.1x |
| 3x2048x2048, patch 64, stride 32 | `reconstruct` | 453.7 ms | 32.1 ms | 14.1x |
| 3x2048x2048, patch 64, stride 32 | `stitch` hann | 460.9 ms | 37.9 ms | 12.2x |

The obvious objection is that the accelerator just uses more cores. It was measured:
forcing torch to 4, 8, 16 and 36 threads on the largest case, the pure path lands between
365 ms and 465 ms, and 36 threads is no better than 8. `F.fold` does not scale at batch
size 1, which is why the kernel exists.

The benchmark compares both paths with `torch.equal` before reporting any timing, and exits
non-zero if they differ.

## The state, in full

1619 tests pass and 1656 are collected, with CI on {Ubuntu, Windows} x {Python 3.12, 3.13,
3.14} on the pure path plus an accelerated job on both systems. The public surface is 20
names, frozen by test with `inspect.signature`.

No external project has consumed the library yet, and that is the criterion the project
itself chose for calling its shape settled. No CUDA path in it has ever executed, in CI or
outside it. Three of the five accelerated wheels have never had their kernel run in CI: the
macOS and aarch64 ones are built and checked, and that is all.

## Reproduction

```
pip install patchcraft
python tools/benchmark.py --markdown
PATCHCRAFT_SWEEP_FULL=1 pytest tests/test_exactness.py
```
